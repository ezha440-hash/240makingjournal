## **Project Statement:**

**What if a city could feel with you?**

This work invites viewers into a near-future Aotearoa where public spaces act as emotional mirrors. In this imagined city, strangers never need to speak to understand one another. Instead, touch-driven digital interfaces allow people to quietly share how they feel, a moment of grief, a flicker of joy, a quiet anxiety. Without names or personal identifiers, anonymous crowds transform into temporary emotional communities connected through shared experience.

The floating, drifting blobs you see on the screen are not random. Each one represents a real person who scanned a QR code and responded to a Google Form about their current emotional state. That self-reported data flows directly into this living visualisation, which updates as new responses arrive. As participation grows, more blobs appear, each carrying only the emotional category someone chose to share, with no identifying details attached.

Rather than presenting data through charts, statistics, or fixed measurements, this work explores alternative approaches to data representation. Emotions are subjective, fluid, and often difficult to quantify. The organic movement, interaction, and colour of the blobs offer a way of seeing data that is collective, anonymous, and constantly changing. By transforming responses into shifting forms, the visualisation challenges the assumption that meaningful data must always be objective, precise, or numerical. Instead, it foregrounds the human experiences behind the dataset and encourages viewers to engage with data as something lived and felt.

The project critically engages with questions surrounding emotional surveillance, digital intimacy, and the ethics of sharing personal information in public space. While the future scenario imagines a more empathetic and connected city, that empathy depends on the continual disclosure of emotional data. The work, therefore, explores the uneasy boundary between care and monitoring, asking whether technologies designed to foster connection might also create new forms of observation and control.

My intention is for visitors to recognise the people behind the data and to reflect on how emotions might be represented, shared, and interpreted in increasingly data-driven environments. Rather than viewing emotional information as something to be measured or monitored, see it as something collective, vulnerable, and worth protecting.
